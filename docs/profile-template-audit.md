# Healthcare practitioner profiles: audit and what it means for our templates

Looked at in a browser, on real profile pages: TherapyRoute, Marham and Oladoc (Pakistan, our market), Headway, Grow Therapy and Zencare (US). Zocdoc, Psychology Today and Counselling Directory blocked automated access (bot checks, regional block), so for those only what their public descriptions say is used, and nothing below depends on them.

## 1. Colour

| Platform | Page | Main brand colour | Accent use |
|---|---|---|---|
| TherapyRoute | white, with a sandy-beige header band | deep petrol teal | filled buttons only; green dot for "online" |
| Marham | light grey page, white cards | navy, with a second green for "video call" | one navy action, one green action |
| Oladoc | white and pale grey | navy; orange only in the logo | outlined and filled navy buttons |
| Headway | pale sky-blue wash, white cards | forest green; near-black for the main button | green for brand, black for "Book session" |
| Grow | white | aubergine / plum | pills and the main button |
| Zencare | white | teal, with a pink-to-orange gradient only on the "take the quiz" prompt | teal for actions, green ticks for "accepting clients" |

What this says:

- **Pages are white or a very pale tint.** None use a saturated background. Colour comes from one calm brand hue (teal, navy, green, plum) used for actions and small markers.
- **Warm neutrals carry the human feel**: sand, oat, stone, a pale sky wash. They are quiet backgrounds for a header band or for cards, never a loud fill.
- **Green and teal mean "good to go"**: accepting clients, verified, available today, fast confirm. Blue and navy mean "trust and medical".
- **Red, orange, yellow and hot pink do not appear** as page colours. They read as alarm or advertising next to a health decision.
- **Dark pages are rare.** Worth keeping as an option, but never the default.

## 2. Type

- Interface text is a clean geometric or humanist **sans** at a generous size (16 to 18 px body, loose line height). Examples: Figtree-like, Inter-like, a Poppins-like geometric on the Pakistan platforms.
- **Serif is a voice, not the chassis.** Only Grow uses one (a display serif for the name and an italic pull-quote); everyone else sets the name in a bold sans.
- The name is the biggest thing on the page, but not enormous (about 32 to 48 px). Nothing is set as a poster headline.
- Section headings are plain, bold and short ("About me", "Services", "Contact"). No kickers, numerals or decoration.

## 3. What is on the page, in order

Every profile follows nearly the same information order, because the person reading is deciding whether this practitioner is safe, suitable and affordable:

1. **Identity block**: face photo, name, credentials or licence after the name (LCSW, MS Clinical Psychology), title, a **Verified** mark, years of experience. Marham and Oladoc add rating and review count.
2. **Availability and the action**: this is the biggest difference from our templates. Headway puts a live mini calendar of time slots next to a "Book session" button. Zencare shows "Soonest available consultation times". Marham and Oladoc show "Available today" and timings on every card. TherapyRoute keeps a Send enquiry card in view. The action never scrolls out of sight.
3. **Quick facts**: session format (online / in person), fee or price range, languages, location, whether new clients are accepted.
4. **Narrative**: a short welcome, then about me, how I work, who I work with. Grow and Headway add "what to expect in a first session".
5. **Specialities and services**, as tags or plain bullet lists. Not tiles, not numbered chapters.
6. **Qualifications**: training, licence type, years, professional memberships.
7. **Location**: address, map link, and the clinic name for each place.
8. **Social proof**: reviews and a satisfaction score (strong on the Pakistan platforms).

## 4. Page architecture (six patterns that actually exist)

1. **Booking rail** (Headway, TherapyRoute, Zencare): identity header, content in a main column, and a sticky card on the right with availability and the action.
2. **Tabbed profile** (Zencare): identity header, a sticky tab bar (About, Specialties, Locations, Fees), content below, booking card on the right.
3. **Directory card** (Marham, Oladoc): a compact single column. Identity and stats at the top, then one card per way of meeting (video call, each clinic) with its fee and next time. Built for phones.
4. **Narrative page** (Grow): big readable name, one headline sentence as a pull-quote, a large portrait, then prose sections with plain headings. Calm and personal.
5. **Practice-site page** (the practitioner's own clinic website): top navigation with section links and a book button, a welcoming hero, services as cards, credentials, location, a closing call to action.
6. **Digital card** (how many practitioners share a link from Instagram or WhatsApp): one narrow centred column with large tap targets. Mobile first.

A seventh, the **side-panel profile** (identity fixed on one side, content scrolling on the other), is a common layout for personal practice sites and works well on wide screens.

## 5. Trust signals

Verified or vetted badge; credentials and licence next to the name; "accepting clients" with a green marker; years of practice; free consultation; price shown up front; next available time; reviews. A visitor should see most of these without scrolling.

## 6. Why the first 20 templates failed

- **They looked like a designer's portfolio, not a clinician's profile.** Magazine mastheads, poster photos, bento tiles, chapter numbers and geometric shapes are agency and editorial patterns. None of the platforms above use them.
- **No trust or availability in the first screen.** Credentials, accepting-clients status, fee, format and next available time were missing or buried. There was no live availability at all, although we have real slots.
- **One skeleton.** Every template was About, then Expertise, then Services, then Journey, with the same kind of tile. Layouts differed in decoration, not in how information is organised.
- **Same background and same fonts.** About 12 of 20 used a cream or oat page, and most pairings were a high-contrast serif with a round sans, so they read alike.
- **Wrong colour language.** Yellow, orange, red, hot pink and black-on-butter (Citrine, Bauhaus Calm, Studio Mono, Saffron) are loud and promotional, not calm.
- **Decorative photo shapes** (arch, blob, offset blocks) distract from the one thing a client wants from a photo: to see a friendly, real face.

## 7. Rules for the redesign

1. **Trust first.** Every template shows credentials, verified status, accepting-clients status, years, session format and fee at the top.
2. **The action is always in reach**, and **real availability** is shown wherever there is room: a mini calendar of open slots, or the next few times.
3. **Calm, healthcare colour** only: teal, blue, green, sage, plum, with warm neutrals. One or two dark options. No red, orange or yellow as page colour.
4. **Seven different architectures**, taken from the real patterns above, not seven decorations on one skeleton.
5. **Twenty different type pairings and twenty different page tones** (white, sky wash, mint, sand, stone, lavender-grey, charcoal, deep teal and so on), so no two templates feel the same.
6. **Plain, readable headings and body text** at generous size. Accessibility first (contrast, tap size, readable fonts such as Atkinson Hyperlegible and Lexend, which were designed for legibility).
7. **No invented content.** The templates only show what the practitioner entered. The only fixed copy is a short "how booking works", which describes our own booking flow.
